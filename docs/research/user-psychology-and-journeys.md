# Fortune-telling users: psychology, first use and reasons to return

Prepared for Jackie Ye · 12 September 2026

**My strongest product hypothesis is a short tarot practice for people who already seek reflection or want to learn.** Their recurring need could sustain a daily product. Someone seeking guidance on one relationship or life event may prefer occasional, deeper readings. Both can be valuable customers, but they require different experiences.

The central psychological hypothesis is: **people return for a repeatable experience of meaning, connection, enjoyment or growing capability. A new prediction is one possible vehicle for that experience.** The first striking reading earns attention; a worthwhile practice earns another visit.

## What this assessment is based on

I reviewed the repository's [market assessment](https://github.com/jackie-ye-0/tarot-ios/blob/8373b3c0557883ff08bfdb4ff01549fd2a4eb829/docs/research/market-opportunity.md), plus the original competitor and demographic companion documents from 9 September. I checked primary survey evidence, recent qualitative research, current official product descriptions and a small recent App Store review sample.

The audience remains English-speaking adults, with no launch country selected. The best population evidence is American. The profiles below are **hypothesised scenarios informed by evidence**, not representative personas, segment-size estimates or real interview quotations. One person can move between several motives. No app was installed or purchased; advertised features have not been tested here. This research does not approve product implementation or any new service.

## 1. What the data actually establish

Pew surveyed 9,593 US adults on 21–27 October 2024. Annual participation was 28% for astrology, 11% for tarot and 6% for fortune tellers; methods overlap. For any of the three, 30% participated annually and 6% at least weekly. Roughly two-thirds of participants primarily sought fun. These describe practices through any channel, not iOS users. [Results](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/), [methodology](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/astrology-tarot-cards-fortune-tellers-methodology/).

Women aged 18–49 had 46% annual astrology participation. Nearly one-quarter of adults aged 18–29 consulted tarot, compared with 6% aged 50–64 and 3% aged 65+. Among LGBT adults, annual astrology and tarot participation were 54% and 33%. These are participation rates within demographic groups, **not the composition of app customers**. [Pew demographic results](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2025/05/21/3-in-10-americans-consult-astrology-tarot-cards-or-fortune-tellers/).

The daily response was 1% for astrology and under 1% each for tarot and fortune tellers, among all US adults. It cannot estimate retention among people who deliberately install a daily app. [Original frequency questions](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/05/PR_2025.05.21_astrology-tarot-cards-fortune-tellers_topline.pdf).

Australia's separate 2024 Pew survey found 11% consulted a fortune teller, horoscope or another method to see the future, with n=2,021. Its question has no annual timeframe and different wording, so it cannot be compared directly with the US annual result. [International questionnaire, Q31c](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2025/05/PR_2025.05.06_spirituality-around-the-world_topline.pdf).

**Evidence gap:** this research did not locate a dependable representative profile of paying English-speaking iOS customers by fortune-telling method, or trustworthy public category retention data. Average income, spending, discovery channel and daily use by category remain unknown. Giving every tradition a precise age, gender and income persona would manufacture certainty.

## 2. The psychology underneath the reading

The following are overlapping motives to investigate, not diagnoses or universal explanations.

### Recognition: “Help me describe what I am feeling.”

A reading can offer vocabulary for an experience that is difficult to explain. Someone may recognise a tension between wanting closeness and needing independence, for example. Their reward is feeling understood. This can be useful to them whether they interpret the reading spiritually, metaphorically or playfully.

**Product implication:** relevant, respectful language matters. A person can enjoy a challenging observation while rejecting ridicule or a deterministic label. Feeling that a passage fits is evidence of subjective resonance, not evidence of predictive accuracy.

### Orientation: “Make this uncertainty easier to think about.”

A person facing an unanswered message, a changing friendship or an unfamiliar life stage may want a coherent way to consider it. Some sincerely seek a prediction; some seek reassurance; some want an alternative interpretation rather than a definite answer.

**Product implication:** inspect what happens after the reading. A new perspective, a concrete next step and repeatedly asking the same unresolved question represent different outcomes. The last can generate high usage without establishing durable value.

### Agency and learning: “Help me discover my own interpretation.”

The user's contribution can be the point of the experience. Drawing a card, connecting its symbols to life and forming an interpretation can feel more satisfying than receiving a finished paragraph.

A CHI 2026 study interviewed 12 existing AI-tarot practitioners in the US, Canada and Mexico. It found uses including interpreting ambiguity, learning and documentation, alongside concern that instant AI answers could displace personal reflection. The selected, qualitative sample does not establish how common either preference is. [Prock et al., full manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11367v1).

**Product implication:** an optional second perspective may suit learners better than automatic answers. Conversely, a person explicitly seeking a quick interpretation may dislike being given homework. These are different intended experiences.

### Ritual and identity: “Give me a small meaningful moment.”

The draw, imagery, pace and repeated time of day can create a boundary around reflection. For an established practitioner, the habit can also express an identity: someone who attends to intuition, spirituality or self-understanding.

A 2026 Moscow study reports entertainment, comfort, community, self-understanding and visual/ritual appeal, using 21 interviews and 15,530 YouTube comments. Only its abstract was accessible; it cannot describe the average English-speaking app user. [Afanasyeva et al.](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/13678779261455104).

**Product implication:** atmosphere may be part of the value. It still needs a satisfying activity beneath it. Whether a digital ritual feels meaningful or artificial needs direct observation.

### Belonging and play: “Give us something interesting to talk about.”

Astrology or cards can supply a low-pressure opening to discuss personality, friendship and relationships. A person can remain sceptical and still enjoy the exchange. A five-person qualitative Co-Star study in a Turkish context found social, comforting and entertainment uses; its narrow selected sample supplies possibilities, not prevalence. [Ersoy, 2023](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/moment/article/1361334).

**Product implication:** the value can occur outside the app, in a conversation. A shared reading may bring somebody in, but retention depends on whether further interactions stay worthwhile.

## 3. Imagine the user in each category

All first-person lines below are imagined. Demographic evidence is strongest for broad astrology and tarot, and weak or absent for the more specific formats. Cultural membership alone does not establish belief or use.

### Western social astrology

*“My friend keeps sending these. I want to know what mine says, and whether it explains us.”*

- **Likely entry to test:** a shared observation, compatibility discussion or an existing interest in horoscopes.
- **First satisfying moment:** recognising something about themselves, laughing, or finding a way to discuss a relationship.
- **Reason to return:** changing social situations, a distinctive daily voice and another conversation to have.
- **Natural cadence hypothesis:** quick daily checks for an engaged subset; occasional compatibility checks for others.
- **Why they leave:** novelty fades, few friends participate, descriptions feel generic, or the tone becomes personally unpleasant.

Co-Star advertises friends' charts, compatibility and daily readings; The Pattern emphasises personality, relationships and life periods. These are different emphases within astrology. [Co-Star](https://www.costarastrology.com/), [The Pattern](https://www.thepattern.com/about-us).

### Reflective astrology

*“Help me start the week with a little more perspective. I like how this person talks to me.”*

- **Entry:** an astrologer, podcast, friend or existing reflective practice.
- **First satisfying moment:** a thoughtful interpretation or audio session that feels relevant and supportive.
- **Return:** familiarity with an author, a regular programme and a chosen quiet moment.
- **Cadence:** daily short practice or a substantial weekly reading. Weekly use can satisfy a paying customer.
- **Departure:** the voice stops resonating, material repeats, the experience becomes too busy, or free alternatives suffice.

CHANI offers daily material, journal prompts, meditations and weekly personalised audio. [Official app description](https://www.chani.com/app).

### Tarot and oracle-style reflection or learning

*“I have a question, or a new deck, and I want help making sense of what I draw.”*

Two users need separating. The **question-led user** wants an interpretation now; the **practice-led user** wants to develop a skill or ritual. A beginner can start with either.

- **Entry:** receiving cards, searching a card meaning, seeing a reading, or bringing a specific concern.
- **First satisfying moment:** a comprehensible card, a new perspective and room for their own response.
- **Return:** another small lesson, a fresh question, an established draw or revisiting an earlier note.
- **Cadence:** plausible daily one-card practice; longer spreads around particular situations.
- **Departure:** interpretations feel interchangeable, learning becomes effortful, prompts demand too much writing, or automation removes the participation they enjoy.

Labyrinthos already supplies lessons, physical and digital reading logs, meanings and reminders. These are existing competitive capabilities, not a newly discovered feature gap. Evidence about tarot should not automatically be generalised to every oracle deck. [App description](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/labyrinthos-tarot-reading/id1155180220).

### Human psychic or tarot-reader consultations

*“I want somebody to understand the details and respond to me.”*

- **Entry:** a meaningful uncertainty, recommendation or interest in a particular reader.
- **First satisfying moment:** responsive dialogue, attention and an interpretation that acknowledges the actual question.
- **Return:** trust in that person and a desire to discuss what happened next.
- **Cadence:** likely tied to developments and life events; daily paid consultation should not be assumed.
- **Departure:** expense, a poor personal connection, generic responses or feeling pressured to continue.

Sanctuary's profiles, availability, favourites and text/voice sessions support this delivery model. Human consultation spans traditions, rather than defining a separate belief system. [Official FAQ](https://www.sanctuaryworld.co/faq/).

### Vedic astrology / Jyotish

*“This framework already matters to me. Help me understand a relationship, transition or its timing.”*

- **Entry:** an existing practice, family discussion, chart curiosity or relationship matching.
- **First satisfying moment:** a credible explanation using the expected tradition and terminology.
- **Return:** calendar checks, developments in the original concern or another conversation with a trusted astrologer.
- **Cadence:** regular calendar/horoscope use and occasional deeper consultations can coexist.
- **Departure:** unconvincing expertise, unexplained calculations, cultural mismatch or poor reader trust.

Astrotalk combines birth charts, matching, calendars and forecasts with consultations. Neither its offerings nor Indian population surveys establish a typical English-speaking diaspora customer. [Official site](https://astrotalk.com/).

### Chinese BaZi

*“Help me understand my tendencies and this period of my life in terms I can follow.”*

- **Entry:** an existing cultural or practitioner connection, a recommendation or curiosity about a life-stage reading.
- **First satisfying moment:** an accessible account of the chart and what its terms mean.
- **Return:** a new life question, a longer-term period or a separate short daily practice.
- **Cadence:** the foundational profile can remain a reference for a long time. Daily opening requires an additional benefit.
- **Departure:** jargon, opaque assumptions or a simplified explanation that feels inauthentic.

FateTell advertises longer-range charts alongside daily rituals and widgets. BaZi, I Ching and ritual tosses remain distinct methods even when bundled in one app. [FateTell](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/fatetell-chinese-astrology/id6752552096). Singapore's National Museum provides historical context for birth-derived charts and matchmaking, not app demographics. [Museum collection](https://www.roots.gov.sg/Collection-Landing/listing/1263437).

### Korean Saju

*“How does this tradition describe me, someone close to me, or the year ahead?”*

- **Entry:** a friend, an existing practice or curiosity about a profile or relationship.
- **First satisfying moment:** recognisable interpretation with clear explanation.
- **Return:** a new period, relationship question or preferred short fortune.
- **Cadence:** occasional, annual and daily modes can serve different needs.
- **Departure:** translation or terminology fails to preserve the meaning, or the initial profile satisfies the curiosity completely.

SaJu offers multiple profiles and daily, monthly and yearly readings. It requires its own customer research; it should not be treated as a cosmetic variant of BaZi. [SaJu listing](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/saju-astrology-horoscope/id366187378).

### Coffee readings and theatrical entertainment

*“Give this moment a little mystery and a story we can enjoy.”*

- **Entry:** an existing coffee ritual, a friend or a playful digital demonstration.
- **First satisfying moment:** atmosphere, anticipation and an engaging interpretation to discuss.
- **Return:** a pleasurable ritual or another occasion for shared storytelling.
- **Cadence:** potentially regular for established enthusiasts, occasional for novelty seekers.
- **Departure:** repeated stories, disruptive advertising or losing the social setting that made the original activity enjoyable.

UNESCO describes Turkish coffee's role in hospitality, shared concerns and fortune-telling. Faladdin offers digital coffee rituals and other modes; its 2026 release notes say real coffee is unnecessary. We should not assume its current onboarding requires a photograph of actual grounds. [UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/turkish-coffee-culture-and-tradition-00645), [Faladdin](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/faladdin-zodiac-love/id1201725025).

### Palmistry, numerology and dream interpretation

These should also be tested separately. **Palmistry:** “What does my hand reveal about me?” An engaging initial reveal does not by itself create a changing daily need. **Numerology:** “What does this name, birth date or period mean?” A personal profile and a calendar-based practice can have different cadences. **Dream interpretation:** “Why did this dream stay with me?” A remembered dream supplies its own irregular trigger; a deliberate dream diary could create more regular use.

Faladdin advertises palm and dream modes, and Astrotalk supplies numerology tools. This review found no robust category-specific demographic or daily-use evidence. [Faladdin](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/faladdin-zodiac-love/id1201725025), [Astrotalk](https://astrotalk.com/).

## 4. A plausible tarot journey, from first encounter to an established practice

This is an imagined journey for the reflective beginner, not a measured funnel. A single user need not complete every stage or visit every day.

| Moment | Imagined thought | What would make the experience worthwhile | Main point of failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before installing | “My thoughts keep going round. That reading my friend shared seemed interesting.” | A clear sample showing what a session is like. | Vague promises, exaggerated certainty or an unclear proposition. |
| First minute | “Can I try this without already knowing tarot?” | One understandable activity and an easy first draw. | Long onboarding, unfamiliar terms or payment before understanding the experience. |
| First reading | “That gives me a different way to look at it.” | Explain the symbol and offer a relevant perspective. Allow disagreement. | Generic praise, a frightening verdict or claims about another person's hidden thoughts. |
| Closing the session | “I know what I want to pay attention to.” | A small optional response: a sentence, intention or action. | A long journal assignment or endless extra readings with no satisfying stopping point. |
| Next available morning | “I liked that little pause. What will I notice today?” | A short practice at a familiar moment; fresh content with a recognisable structure. | The same advice repackaged, too many reminders or too much effort. |
| End of the week | “I can see what I was thinking, and what actually changed.” | Revisit notes, including readings that did not fit; notice learning and choices. | Treating every outcome as confirmation or providing no useful continuity. |
| Following weeks | “This is part of how I reflect, and I understand the cards better.” | A practice that remains enjoyable and useful, with optional depth. | Habit persists without benefit, learning plateaus or the original question resolves. |
| A missed week | “I would like to pick this up again.” | A welcoming return with their chosen context intact. | Guilt, lost progress or an assumption that missed days mean failure. |

The corresponding question-led journey may be shorter: **uncertainty → reading → follow-up after a development → resolution → no current need**. That can be a successful experience. It does not automatically justify a daily subscription.

## 5. What could make someone return every day?

For the reflective or learning segment, I would test five ingredients together:

1. **A familiar moment.** Attach a brief practice to an existing routine, such as morning coffee. A general habit study supports stable-context repetition, but did not study tarot apps or prove retention. [Lally et al.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674).
2. **A recurring benefit.** Each session gives a useful perspective, enjoyable pause or small learning gain. A notification only reminds someone of that benefit.
3. **Freshness within a recognisable structure.** A new symbol or question, delivered in an experience that requires little relearning.
4. **Participation and continuity.** The person adds their own meaning, then can revisit it. Any saved context would require a separate privacy/storage decision before building.
5. **A satisfying end.** They finish with a sense that the session was enough and can return willingly. Endless prompts to redraw are a different behaviour to a valued daily practice.

Daily usage should not be manufactured for every category. A static profile may be a worthwhile one-off purchase, a creator's weekly reading may support a subscription, and a human consultation may be worth paying for only when needed.

## 6. A small check against actual app reviews

I inspected the first ten entries in each app's most-recent US Apple review feed on 12 September 2026: 30 reviews total, without keyword filtering. The feeds returned 50 each, but the remaining entries were not analysed. These are self-selected, unverified accounts, not complaint prevalence or average-user estimates.

- **CHANI, inspected dates 6–9 September 2026:** one reviewer describes a family referral, free use, a trial, then paying because the weekly forecast alone is worthwhile. Another describes a Sunday routine. A separate reviewer finds email frequency excessive. [Review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1532791252/sortBy=mostRecent/json).
- **Labyrinthos, 18 June–10 September 2026:** a long-term user describes discovering its educational website, looking up meanings and doing occasional readings away from their physical cards. Others value learning or report intrusive upselling and inconsistent explanations. [Review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1155180220/sortBy=mostRecent/json).
- **Co-Star, 29 August–9 September 2026:** a positive review reports daily relevance; another likes challenging readings but finds some wording invalidating. One critical reviewer describes friends influencing premium purchase, followed by dissatisfaction with tone. These accounts do not establish developer intent. [Review feed](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=1264782561/sortBy=mostRecent/json).

These examples strengthen specific interview questions: whom did the user trust, what routine developed, how much automation did they want, and did the product leave them feeling understood? They cannot tell us how many users share those experiences. The linked feeds change over time.

## 7. What I recommend testing next

Recruit by **recent behaviour**, initially prioritising adults who already draw cards or use reflection/learning tools several times a week. Include some who stopped using an app. The demographic data can help diversify recruitment, but should not replace evidence of an existing need.

Compare two concrete promises before building:

- **Guided reflection:** “One card, one useful perspective, one small intention.” The benefit is an accessible reflective pause; the challenge is proving value beyond a pleasant generic message.
- **Tarot learning companion:** “Interpret the card yourself, then explore another perspective.” The benefit is growing capability; the cost is more user effort and competition from established educational tools.

My first choice is to test guided reflection with optional explanation, while deliberately including learners to discover whether they want a different product. A highly predictive experience would attract different expectations and should be treated as a consequential positioning choice, not quietly blended into this promise.

For interviews, ask about the **last actual session**: what happened just before opening, how they discovered the tool, what they entered, how the reading affected them, what they did afterwards, why they next returned, and why they paid or stopped. Where comfortable, ask them to demonstrate with a nonsensitive example.

A subsequent, separately authorised 14-day concept trial could record chosen routine, completed sessions, prompted versus self-initiated returns, reported usefulness and reasons for skipping. Fourteen days is a practical observation window, not a validated habit-formation deadline. Track the denominator as everyone who started, including dropouts. Progress would mean repeated voluntary use with a concrete benefit and a reason to choose this over the current alternative. If return depends entirely on reminders or novelty, revise the proposition. Willingness to pay still requires its own test.

The immediate decision is **which experience we want people to seek again: a prediction, a conversation, a learning activity or a meaningful pause**. That choice should lead the app's design.
